# Validation results

Solver: `openai` (gpt-4o-mini) · k ∈ [1, 5, 25, 100, 500] · 5 independent runs per k · generated by `python -m validation.run` in 1377s.

> **Real-model curves.** Each agent is one `gpt-4o-mini`-class call at temperature 1.0 with a distinct seed; verdicts come from the workload's verifier/judge; the fuzzy workload's verdicts are a keyword rubric proxy (`source=judge`, confidence 0.5) until a human reviews them.

## Findings

- **Replay ablation reproduces the prospective dose-response on all three workloads** once both are averaged over the same tasks (pooled retrospective vs prospective: 1.00/1.00 objective, 0.40/0.40 semi-objective at k≥100, 1.00/1.00 fuzzy at k≥25). Replay fidelity was identical on every run. One large run plus replay stands in for a k-sweep here.
- **The objective workload saturates at k=1.** A current small model solves these four programming tasks 99–100% of the time per agent, so the swarm adds nothing; the curve is flat at 1.0 and the fitted per-agent success rate is 0.995. Harder tasks are needed before this workload says anything about k.
- **On the semi-objective workload the consolidation step, not the agent count, is the bottleneck.** Per task at k=500: agents were right 100% (author-birth) and 54% (moon-count) of the time and the plurality vote succeeded; they were right 3–4% (capital-river) and 1% (element-discovery) of the time and the vote failed, because a confidently wrong answer ("the Bosphorus") wins the plurality. P(success) is flat at 0.40 from k=25 to k=500: 20× more agents bought nothing. The fix is a better consolidator (a verifier-aware or minority-preserving one), not more agents.
- **The fuzzy workload's keyword proxy saturates by k≈5–25** (knee 25 empirically, 6 from the fit) and has not been human-reviewed, so its curve is a statement about the proxy, not about report quality. Per-agent quality is not measured on this workload.
- **Cost scales linearly with k while value does not.** A fuzzy swarm at k=500 costs $0.142 for the same outcome as k=5 at $0.0014, a 100× spend for nothing measurable; the semi-objective k=500 run wasted 93% of its tokens by the reachability measure.
- **The dedup-gate experiment is uninformative with this solver** (one identical claim per agent, no suppressions); it needs agents that express distinct approaches and honour the advice, as the simulated solver does.
- **Operational:** the organisation's daily request cap (10,000/day per model on this tier) is below the protocol's ~11,000 calls, so the objective workload ran on `gpt-4o-mini` and the other two on `gpt-4.1-mini`; each workload is single-model. Total spend across all attempts: $1.28.

_Total model spend for this run, including two aborted attempts: $1.28 over 12,108 generations._

## Reading these numbers

- Everything is P(success) with a CI, never a mean alone: swarm search is heavy-tailed and a team that sizes off a mean under-provisions.
- The prospective/retrospective gap is the honest cost of replay ablation. Where it is small, one large run plus replay replaces a k-sweep.
- The fuzzy workload's verdicts are self-reported rubric proxies until a human reviews them (`swarmscope review`); treat its curve as a lower bound on uncertainty, not on quality.
